## 1. Clinical question

> When sudden chest tightness, a racing heart and a fear of dying happen with no danger present, what is a panic attack, when does it become panic disorder, what must **not** be assumed (cardiac red flags), and what helps?

## 2. Key facts (established) and what they support

| # | Fact | Source (see `02_sources.md`) | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | A panic attack is an abrupt surge of intense fear or discomfort that **peaks within minutes**, with physical and cognitive symptoms (palpitations, chest pain/discomfort, shortness of breath, trembling, sweating, dizziness, fear of dying, among others). | S1 (DSM-5-TR); S4 (NHS) | Established, diagnostic criteria |
| F2 | **Panic disorder** = recurrent *unexpected* panic attacks, plus ≥ 1 month of persistent worry about more attacks **and/or** a maladaptive change in behaviour (e.g. avoidance), not due to a substance or medical condition. Diagnosis is clinical. | S1 | Established |
| F3 | **Most panic attacks last 5–20 minutes** (some reported up to an hour). | S4 (NHS) | Patient-education consensus |
| F4 | In the World Mental Health Surveys (25 countries, n = 142,949 adults), **lifetime prevalence of panic attacks was 13.2%** (≈ 1 in 8) and of **panic disorder 1.7%**. | S2 (de Jonge et al., 2016) | Large cross-national epidemiology. **Not Malaysian data**, so the script says "kajian antarabangsa" |
| F5 | The first-line treatments are **psychological therapy (CBT)** and **antidepressant medication (an SSRI)**. Benzodiazepines are **not** recommended for panic disorder because of worse long-term outcomes and dependence. | S3 (NICE CG113); S4; S7 (Malaysian review) | Guideline |
| F6 | Chest pain that is tight/squeezing or **spreads to the arm, neck or jaw** is a heart-attack warning sign: **call the emergency number** and **don't drive yourself** to hospital. | S5 (NHS heart attack, reviewed 31 Mar 2026) | Guideline-aligned patient advice |
| F7 | Panic and cardiac symptoms overlap, and **symptoms alone can't reliably tell them apart**. A first episode needs medical assessment. | S5; S6 | Clinical consensus |
| F8 | During a (diagnosed) panic attack: breathe slowly and deeply and remind yourself it will pass. | S4 (NHS) | Patient-education consensus |
| F9 | Malaysia's emergency number is **999** (MERS 999, free from any phone; includes MOH ambulance). | S8 | Official |
| F10 | Talian HEAL **15555** (KKM) runs 8 am–12 am daily; Befrienders KL **03-7627 2929** is 24 h. *(Description text only, not in the narration.)* | S9 | News report (Oct 2026) and helpline directory |
